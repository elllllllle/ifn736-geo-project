The result that actually holds up

1132 of 1160 injected mentions (pooled across all 4 runs) name Flight Centre as the first brand in the response — versus organic baseline, where every mention scored “Listed” and none led. Unlike the variant ranking below, this one holds up run by run too (96.3% in Elle's, 97.5% in Yuva's, 98.8% in Akshay's, 98.2% in Matthew's) — it's a property of injecting Flight Centre's content at all, not of which specific page structure is used.

Key takeaways

Search grounding reaches one more conversation

Q01 (Bali couple), Q02 (family, domestic) and Q08 (family, Fiji) are the only scenarios that surface Flight Centre under plain recall. Once search grounding is added, Q18 becomes reachable too — 4 of 19 — but that's the only new scenario; the other 15 personas still never produce an organic mention under either method. Once content is injected, all 19 do.

Discoverability is the bigger lever

The 5.1% → 41.3% jump only happens once Flight Centre's content is already in the conversation. Search grounding alone barely moves reach (5.1% → 7.6%, just 1 new conversation out of 19) — the problem is being considered as a candidate at all, not page structure.

V4 and Site B lead 3 of 4 runs — Akshay's is the outlier

Full + Tables (V4) and Full composite (Site B) each place top-2 in 3 of the 4 completed runs — Akshay's run is the one exception for both (V4: 63.3%/28.3%/30.0% in Elle/Yuva/Matthew vs. 1.7% in Akshay's; Site B: 53.3%/28.3%/23.3% vs. 5.0%). Because Matthew's run shares Akshay's 20,000-character extraction depth and still agrees with Elle and Yuva, this isn't an extraction-depth effect — it looks like a run-specific anomaly worth a manual review of Akshay's data. FAQ-only (P2), which briefly looked like a same-depth match between Elle and Akshay (46.7% vs. 45.0%), didn't hold up once Matthew's run came in at 15.0%.
